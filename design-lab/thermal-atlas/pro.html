Chapter I — the professional edition

Making heat predictable.

Percutaneous thermal ablation treats small liver tumors — but when one needle isn't enough, clinicians plan overlapping burns on 2D CT slices and recurrence reaches 39.1% at five years. This chapter documents a PhD spent fixing that.

The research proceeds along three fronts. First, multi-needle trajectory optimization — comparing exhaustive and learning-based placement methods while respecting vital structures, with search-space reduction and deep-learning ablation-volume estimation. Second, intraoperative replanning: tracking inserted needles, estimating the partly treated volume, and adapting the plan as the procedure unfolds. Third, an ergonomic planning interface validated with the clinicians who actually use it.

The simulation work began during Master internships split between the MEC-Lab at TU Darmstadt and the IMAGeS group at ICube, Strasbourg — taking over CryoTrack, a planning and navigation system for cryoablation, and pushing its thermal simulation onto the GPU. The result, HEAT, computes ablation outcomes in under a second; C-NCA, a chained neural cellular automaton, estimates twenty-five minutes of treatment at up to 476 frames per second with barely 12,210 learned parameters — fast enough to sit inside an interactive optimization loop.
