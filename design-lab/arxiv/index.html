Abstract Percutaneous thermal ablation destroys small liver tumors with needles and heat — but when one needle is not enough, clinicians plan overlapping ablations manually, and recurrence follows. This site presents the professional work of J. Mehtali (PhD, ICube): GPU-accelerated ablation simulation, multi-needle trajectory optimization, and intraoperative replanning — together with Appendix B, a personal appendix documenting games, 3D artifacts, and prior trades, produced after hours with equivalent rigor and softer deadlines.

2Methods

Research proceeds along three axes. (i) Multi-needle trajectory optimization: exhaustive and learning-based placement under anatomical constraints, with search-space reduction and deep-learning ablation-volume estimation. (ii) Intraoperative replanning: tracking of inserted needles, estimation of the partly treated volume, and plan adaptation as the procedure unfolds. (iii) Ergonomic planning interfaces: a GUI for planning, tracking and replanning, validated through usability studies with clinical experts and novices.

Funding: ITI HealthTech doctoral program, ED269 MSII. Supervisors: C. Essert, J. Verde.

Appendix B — the human behind the paper

B.1After-hours artifacts

When something annoying needs solving, the author builds tools for it — small utilities and LLM experiments (side quests.txt). The rest of the time goes to games and 3D: Sensha, a real-time tank strategy born for the Baccalauréat and reborn in Godot; Airship VR, a puzzle-survival for Meta headsets; Notre-Dame, medieval architecture reconstructed in 3D for a Strasbourg museum; and Nacht der Teddybären, a teddy-zombie survival built in Unity.

Full documentation of the workshop — with gameplay videos, Blender assets and museum renders — is provided in Appendix B (personal edition).
